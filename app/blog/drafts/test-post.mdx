## Who Would Have a Blog?
#### Oct 29, 2025

"Everything is a work in progress"

This phrase is commonly found in developer portfolios of people who want to come across as passionate and thoughtful, but when interpreted a different way, it instead seems immature. I'd like to introduce (The Cult of Done manifesto)[https://medium.com/@bre/the-cult-of-done-manifesto-724ca1c2ff13], a set of ideas written some time ago. It introduces several concepts aimed at giving inspiration or aiding productivity, all of which are debatable. But, there's one I want to mention, namely, the final one: "Done is the engine of more."

This seems true, and I'd say it is. If one tries to plan out an infinite amount of projects or tasks in their head, nothing will ever get done. Now, as we approach 0, it would become easier and easier! At the lower numbers, other factors come into play; maybe some people prefer a couple projects, while some prefer just one. Either way, the effective maximum is low. To say that everything is a work in progress is to say you either haven't done very many things in your life, or that you are dividing your attention across an insane amount of things.

There's a sense of maturity associated with being done, in both the pride given by being "done" and having accomplishments, and also from the non-chalantness of moving on from a project. But there's a great sense of passion associated with everything being in the works, with another version nearly out, like an inventor in a workshop chock full of all of their doohickeys. In that vision lies the promise of eccentricity and genius, and I believe this is what people aim to invoke with the phrase, "Everything is a work in progress."

I have maybe done a disservice to that phrase by taking it so literally. If we take a more nebulous interpretation of the phrase, we can find something more desirable. Just as the inventor in the thingamajig-laden workshop, not everything is kept at the same importance. Things fade into obscurity, gather dust, and their odds of being rediscovered gets low, but never to 0. In this way, everything is a work in progress, but in a more practical sense.

Let's compare this to the opposite: a trim and tidy workshop, with every tool in its place and every invention filed away as a precise schematic. It invokes efficiency and maturity, someone who knows what they're doing. Knowing what they're doing? That one's debatable, but the idea itself is significant. It's difficult to claim to know what you're doing, because what if you don't! Oh, the tragedy. If you know what you are doing, you risk lacking humility. If you don't know what you are doing, you risk lacking efficiency.

A lot of ideas have been said in regards to these kinds of statements. Here's a list of all of them:
- immature
- maturity
- non-chalantness
- pride
- having accomplishments
- passion
- eccentricity
- genius
- efficiency
- humility

To trim it down a bit, we can get rid of maturity, which is, at least how I used it here, largely a function of the other ones. Then, we can get rid of genius and eccentricity, simply because I don't see those as universally appealing. Then, non-chalantness, often confused with maturity, is more often negative. And pride is a sin. Finally, having accomplishments is derivitive of efficiency, so we can drop that.

Our final list:
- passion
- efficiency
- humility

That's really short! But I think I like it. Let's compare it to the original phrase "Everything is a work in progress". It shows passion, it doesn't show efficiency, and shows humility. Conveniently, this lines up with my original suspicions. Can we do better?

Maybe not. Efficiency is the ratio between input to output, and passion and humility are personality traits. But then how do we ever show it? It seems the most reliable way to show efficiency is through its child, having accomplishments. But maybe efficiency is a child of something else.
